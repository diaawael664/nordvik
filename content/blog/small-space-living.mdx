Small homes don't need small furniture. They need fewer pieces, chosen carefully, that let light and floor show through.

## Raise everything off the floor

Furniture on slim legs shows more floor, and a room with visible floor reads as larger. Avoid skirted sofas and solid plinths in small rooms.

## Let pieces do two jobs

- A **stool** that's also a side table and a plant stand.
- A **desk** shallow enough for a hallway alcove.
- A **nightstand** with a closed drawer, so the surface stays clear.

## Use the height of the room

Tall, open shelving draws the eye up and holds far more than a wide, low unit, on a fraction of the floor.

> Choose one generous piece rather than three small ones. A single large rug or a proper sofa makes a small room feel deliberate.

## Keep the palette quiet

Light walls, pale wood and one or two deeper accents. In a small space every color counts, so let a single piece carry the color.
