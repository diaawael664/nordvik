The first Vinge prototype cracked in the steam box. So did the second. By the fifth, we'd learned more about oak than in the previous ten years.

## One length of oak

The idea was simple: a single piece of oak bent into a continuous curve that becomes the legs, the arms and the frame for the seat. No joints in the curve, nothing to loosen over time.

Doing it was not simple. Oak bends beautifully when it's steamed, but only within a narrow window of moisture and temperature. Too dry and it splits; too wet and it springs back.

## What eleven prototypes taught us

- **Grain matters more than thickness.** We now select every blank by hand for straight, unbroken grain.
- **Slow is faster.** Bending over four minutes instead of one cut our breakage rate from one in three to one in thirty.
- **The shell sets the comfort.** We reshaped the upholstered shell seven times to get the recline right.

## Signed by its maker

Every Vinge frame is still bent by one of three joiners, and each signs the underside. If it ever needs repairing, it goes back to the person who made it.
