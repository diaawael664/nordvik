Oiled oak isn't sealed under a plastic layer. The oil soaks into the wood, which is why it feels warm and silky, and why it can be repaired when it gets marked. Look after it a little and it gets better with age.

## Everyday care

- Wipe spills straight away with a damp cloth, then dry the surface.
- Use coasters and trivets. Heat and standing water leave the deepest marks.
- Dust with a soft, dry cloth. Avoid spray polishes. They leave a film the oil can't get through.

## The yearly re-oil

Once or twice a year, or whenever the wood looks dry and pale:

1. Clean the surface with a little soap flake solution and let it dry completely.
2. Rub in a thin layer of the care oil with a lint-free cloth, working with the grain.
3. Leave it for 15 minutes, then wipe off **all** the excess. Oil left on the surface turns sticky.
4. Let it cure overnight before using the surface again.

<Callout title="Water rings and light scratches">
  Most marks disappear with a light sanding using 240-grit paper along the grain, followed by a fresh coat of oil.
</Callout>

> The marks are part of the story. A table that's been used for twenty years should look like it.
